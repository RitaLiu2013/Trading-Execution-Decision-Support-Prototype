Liquidity describes how easily an asset can be traded in meaningful size without causing a large change in its price.

A liquid market generally allows buyers and sellers to transact more easily, while a less liquid market may make execution more difficult.

The bid-ask spread is the difference between the best available buying price and selling price.

A wider bid-ask spread can increase the implicit cost of trading.

Available liquidity at a given price may be limited.

If an order is larger than the quantity available at the best quoted price, different parts of the order may execute at different prices.

Large orders can therefore require access to liquidity across multiple price levels or trading venues.

Market impact refers to the effect that executing an order can have on market prices.

The relationship between order size, available liquidity, spread, market conditions, and execution strategy can influence execution quality.

Liquidity should therefore be considered together with trade size, market conditions, and execution objectives rather than interpreted using a single metric.
