Execution risk is the risk that the actual implementation of a trading decision differs materially from the intended execution outcome.

Execution outcomes can be affected by changes in market prices while an order is being executed.

Limited liquidity can make it harder to execute the desired quantity at attractive prices.

Large orders relative to available liquidity may face greater market impact or require execution across multiple prices.

Higher market volatility can increase uncertainty around the prices available during execution.

Execution delays can also affect the difference between the price observed when a trading decision is made and the prices eventually achieved.

An order may also be only partially executed if sufficient liquidity is not available under the chosen execution conditions.

Execution quality therefore cannot be evaluated only by asking whether an order was completed.

Relevant analysis may include execution prices, benchmarks, trade size, liquidity conditions, timing, market impact, and whether the intended quantity was executed.

Execution-risk analysis should support decision-making rather than automatically imply that one execution strategy is optimal in every situation.
